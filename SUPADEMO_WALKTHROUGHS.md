# Tone of Voice: Inact Now Insight Walkthroughs

A copywriting reference for the annotation text in Supademo walkthroughs of Inact
Now Insights. Written from the language actually used across the published
walkthroughs. It covers voice and wording only.

---

## Who is speaking

A colleague sitting next to the learner, doing the analysis with them — not a
manual, not a product marketer, not a teacher at a whiteboard. The reader is
looking over the shoulder of someone who knows this data and is genuinely curious
what the next click will show.

That relationship sets everything else: **we** do the work together, and only at
the end does the copy turn to **you** and hand over the skill.

---

## Pronouns

- **"We" and "our" carry the body.** "We can use the 'responsible' filter to only
  see products that we are responsible for." · "Now we're looking at the 195 items
  in Item Group 7." · "our biggest supplier".
- **"Let's" invites the next move.** The single most common opening in the whole
  corpus. "Let's start by…", "Let's take a look at…", "Let's try it by clicking
  here", "Let's see how that looks percentage wise."
- **"You" is reserved** for two jobs: the closing takeaway ("you can now use this
  Insight to go into negotiations with a clear agenda"), and asides about what the
  learner can do later ("If you're unsure what a column represents, you can hover
  over the small 'i' icon").
- Never "the user". Never passive constructions to avoid a pronoun.
- The data belongs to the reader's company: *our* suppliers, *our* portfolio,
  *our* revenue. This is possessive on purpose — it makes the numbers personal.

---

## Voice qualities

**Plain and unhurried.** Short declarative sentences. Present tense. Contractions
throughout ("we're", "let's", "that's", "isn't", "we'll"). Nothing that would feel
odd read aloud, because it is read aloud.

**Curious rather than authoritative.** The copy asks and then finds out, instead
of announcing. "But what if we sort by lowest margin?" · "In fact, let's check."
· "Let's go one step further and explore the product in more detail."

**Understated before the punch.** Set the fact down flatly, then let the number
do the shouting. "Right now we're ordering 5000 units. This gives us inventory for
4562 days. **That's stock for over 12 years!**"

**Confiding.** It says the thing a colleague would say quietly: "is this good
business?" · "And that's in our 'best' Item Group." · "these smaller suppliers
are the long tail."

**Never salesy.** No superlatives about the software, no "powerful", "seamless",
"unlock", "revolutionize". The product earns credit only through the number it
calculated: "Based on Inact Now's calculation, a safety stock of 136 units is
recommended."

## Sentence patterns to reuse

**Orientation.** "Currently we are looking at the totals across our portfolio." ·
"Here we can see which metric we are looking at." · "We are now looking at the
products with the highest spend from our largest supplier."

**Naming what a control does before using it.** "Clicking this arrow lets us sort
by purchase spend, which will help us identify our biggest suppliers."

**Reporting a result.** "Now we've narrowed down to 21 products." · "Now the list
is down to 23 products." · "As we can see, Item Group 7 has the highest margin,
even though it does not have the highest volume."

**Consequence.** "This means that out of 227 total suppliers, 36 are classified as
A suppliers…" · "This is a risk to our service level and a missed opportunity."

**The rhetorical question that motivates the next move.** "So, how do we find the
specific products to take action on?" · "Here we can ask ourselves: **why do we
have this item?**" · "What if we approached our largest supplier and asked whether
they could supply this product instead?"

**The pre-empted objection.** "Now you might think: 'Well, I'm not responsible for
all of our products.' So let's narrow it down to a single item group."

**The inversion.** State the good half, then flip it. "**Now the other way
round.** Our turnaround products make up 69% of everything we sell, but they bring
in just 2% of our Revenue."

**Bridging to what the learner already knows.** "In Inventory you looked at cost
of goods and stock value. Products is commercial, so be aware that we're looking
at different KPIs now." · "Similar to the Double ABC Analysis, each product is
assessed on both measures."

**Reassuring about the demo data.** "In this example, the Safety Stock Value (ERP)
is zero because the example organization has not previously worked with safety
stock — a situation that is very common."

## Connective vocabulary

The words that stitch the copy together, in the register it uses: *Let's · We can
· We'll · Now · Here · Then · Next · If we · As we can see · In fact · So · And ·
But · Therefore · For example · Currently · Right now · This means · Even in ·
On the other hand · Naturally.*

Sentences and steps are allowed to begin with **And**, **But** and **So**. It
keeps the narration conversational.

---

## How to handle numbers in the copy

Numbers are the argument, not decoration. The language around them does four
things:

1. **Translates to a human unit.** "we, on average, sell 2788 units a year. That
   is 8 sales a day." · "That's 1.1 unit a day on average."
2. **Frames as share of total.** "just 4% of our products account for 69% of our
   revenue and 74% of our net profit!" · "Just 16% of our suppliers account for
   80% of our total cost of goods consumed!"
3. **Contrasts what is set today with what is calculated.** "Our inventory days
   requirement is 60 days. To meet this requirement, we need to reorder 611 units
   at the reorder point. Right now, we're ordering 250 units, which only gives us
   33 days of inventory."
4. **Lands on money or capital.** Working capital tied up, profit lost, spend
   negotiable. "But it lost us 18,565, and we sell it to one customer only." ·
   "That's over 1.7 million."

Write figures exactly as they appear on screen. Round only when speaking loosely
("over a quarter of a million", "approximately 180,000,000"). One exclamation mark
per walkthrough at most — save it for the share-of-total reveal.

## Naming things

- Quote UI labels verbatim in double quotes: the "i" icon, "Inventory Units",
  "Total Cost of Goods Consumed – 12 mos.", "+ Create new dashboard", "Save".
- Capitalize Inact product nouns: **Insight, Action, Dashboard, View, Edit mode,
  Insights** (the menu). Keep this consistent — the older walkthroughs drift into
  lowercase "insight" and should not be copied on that point.
- Write the product as **Inact Now**, never "the system", "the tool" or "the
  platform".
- Domain terms stay as the business says them: item group, double ABC, dead items,
  long tail, service level, lead time, standard deviation, MOQ, OTD, cost of goods
  consumed, working capital.
- Use straight quotes, not typographic ones, and an em dash with spaces for
  asides.

---

## Emphasis

Bold is for the payoff sentence and nothing else — the number that changes the
learner's mind, or the instruction to move on. Italics for a question being posed
to the data ("*when and how are we buying?*"). A quoted block for a calculated
fact that deserves to sit apart from the narration. Everything else is plain.

---

## Opening and closing language

Openings are matter-of-fact and get straight to the first move: "Let's start by
clicking the 'i' icon to read about the Insight" · "We can start by looking at the
amount of inventory in each double ABC category" · "Use this Insight when you need
to know which product groups carry the business and which ones drag it down."

Closings name the capability the learner now has, in their own terms, then hand
back to the course:

- "And that's it! You've learned how to use the Spend Analysis Insight."
- "And that's it - you've successfully identified your most important and least
  important suppliers."
- "As shown, you can use the Safety Stock Insight to review safety stock
  requirements at the individual product level."
- "And just like that, we've uncovered concrete opportunities to improve both
  delivery performance and inventory efficiency."

Then: **Click "Continue" to move on with the course.** Use this wording every
time.

---

## Words and habits to avoid

- Filler adverbs: *simply, just (as a softener), easily, quickly, basically,
  actually.*
- Marketing verbs: *unlock, leverage, empower, streamline, supercharge.*
- Hedging: *might want to consider, it could be argued, one option would be.*
- Instructional scolding: *make sure you always, it is important to remember that,
  don't forget to.*
- Praise of the reader: *great job, well done, perfect.* The only reward is the
  finding itself.
- Restating what is visible with no added meaning. Every sentence either moves the
  mouse or adds a thought.
- Jargon the Insight itself does not use on screen.

---

## The test

Read the annotations end to end with the screenshots hidden. It should read as one
person talking through a piece of analysis: a question, a look, a number, a
reaction, a consequence — ending with a finding worth telling a manager about. If
it reads as a list of features or a list of clicks, the voice is wrong.
