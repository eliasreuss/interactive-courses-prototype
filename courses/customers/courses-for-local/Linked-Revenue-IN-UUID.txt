[id:3/topic: Healthy and Unhealthy Complexity]

slide_id: a19d7b68-8f6f-4d74-9816-07d6c203d5b1
index_title: C Items Sold to Protect Customers
title: C Items Sold to Protect Customers
type: main
---
In the Inventory and Products courses, many C items looked like obvious candidates to reduce or remove. But we were looking at the product on its own. Before making that decision, we need to know who buys it.

The **C Items Sold to Protect Customers** Insight shows you C items that one or more of your most important customers buys.

Low revenue, few order lines, but a customer you do not want to lose.
![image/size:large/align:center/frame:none](customers-linked-revenue/C-To-Protect-Screenshot.png)
---

index_title: Healthy Complexity - End to End Walkthrough
title: Healthy Complexity - End to End Walkthrough
type: main
---
We have now found C items that important customers buy. That changes the decision.

Looking at the product alone, we may want to remove it. Looking at the customer relationship, we may decide that keeping it is necessary. This is **healthy complexity**. Complexity that exists because it supports customers that matter to the business.

Now we can take the next step and see these relationships in End2End. Here, we can connect the customers to the products they buy and then follow those products back to the suppliers behind them.
![image/size:large/align:center/frame:none](customers-linked-revenue/Healthy-Complexity-Walkthrough.png)

Some products may only exist because a small group of weak customers buy them.

This is where we need to question whether the products, inventory and customer relationship still make sense.
