[id:2/topic: Why Customers Matter]

slide_id: d0528d99-676d-48ef-9303-7930fa1a5d70
index_title: It Starts With Your Customers
title: It Starts With Your Customers
type: main
layout: standard-2-col
---
Every part of the value chain exists to serve a customer.

What your customers need shapes the products in your assortment. Those products decide the inventory you hold, and the inventory decides which suppliers you depend on.

**When you understand your customers, you understand why the rest of the value chain looks the way it does.**
![image/size:large/align:right/frame:none](customers-intro/Customer-Chain.png)
---

slide_id: ed91db1a-4044-4cad-adc5-4a95506ba9e8
index_title: Each Part Only Tells Part of the Story
title: Each Part Only Tells Part of the Story
type: main
layout: standard-2-col
---
It is easy to assess each part of the business on its own.

Product performance tells you how a product performs. Customer profitability tells you what you earn on a customer. Both are useful, but neither tells you how they depend on each other.

Before you remove a weak product, you need to ask:

**Who relies on it?**
![image/size:large/align:right/frame:none](customers-intro/Three-Views.png)
---

slide_id: d94dc254-0f89-4467-b726-ed62eaad4609
index_title: Which Complexity Is Worth Keeping?
title: Which Complexity Is Worth Keeping?
type: main
layout: standard-2-col
---
Serving customers creates complexity. Every product, every item in stock and every supplier relationship adds work.

Some of that complexity supports valuable business. Some of it adds cost without enough return.

The challenge is to tell the two apart.
![image/size:large/align:right/frame:none](customers-intro/Complexity.png)
