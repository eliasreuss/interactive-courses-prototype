The key question is simple: **Are we introducing the right products?**

title: What Happens After Launch?
type: main
layout: standard-2-col
---
Introducing a new product is only the beginning. Once it is in the portfolio, we need to find out whether it actually develops into a strong product.

Exact failure rates vary by industry and by how failure is measured, but estimates often place new product failure at around **30 to 50 percent,** with some industries reporting even higher rates.

That makes early follow up critical. The sooner we see that a new product is falling behind, the sooner we can decide what to do about it.

**If we do not follow up on new products, today’s new introductions can become tomorrow’s dead stock.**
![video/size:large/align:right/frame:none](https://pub-3c39fac4c588469d9bf40bf9e56bdbaf.r2.dev/Products/Failing-Products.mp4)

[id:2/topic: NPI Framework]

slide_id: e2664aa4-81d8-49d8-b470-f2f8caeed754
index_title: How Is a New Product Performing?
title: How Is a New Product Performing?
type: main
---
In Inact, we use the NPI Framework to track how new products develop after launch. The first thing we look at is performance.

The exact criteria and thresholds can vary. Common examples include revenue, volume and actual sales compared with forecast.

Based on the chosen criteria, each new product falls into one of three groups.
![image/size:large/align:center/frame:none](learn/images/272/57b27a91ffca4457dac781c4b0d577da9b071d2dd30dba59109e8929ce7f1f23)

index_title: The First 12 Months
title: The First 12 Months
type: main
---
Performance is only one part of the picture. We also need to consider how long the product has been in the portfolio.

The NPI Framework follows new products through their first 12 months and divides them into three age groups. As the product gets older, we expect more from its performance.
![image/size:large/align:center/frame:none](learn/images/269/a4fb8110e6db77877bfd3fe026dab03910c114e0aa74406dd3f8abd593ab7d58)

slide_id: cc3f441d-88ae-4bbd-bb63-bee6684ba2c5
index_title: The NPI Framework
title: The NPI Framework
type: main
---
Now we bring the two dimensions together. The framework combines a product’s age with its performance at that point in time.

As the product moves through its first 12 months, we continue to assess whether it is **On track, Promising or Needs attention.** This makes it easy to see which new products are developing as expected and which ones require action.
![image/size:large/align:center/frame:none](learn/images/271/e3fee22240eb576eb6433b7bde1b42430bd28a922a65e5352b39ce0ef978a98e)

index_title: Improve NPI Success Rate
title: Improve NPI Success Rate
type: main
---
The purpose of the NPI Framework is to raise the success rate of new products, so a larger share of launches reach their targets and become a healthy part of the assortment.

Transparency and proactivity make this possible. With a shared view of how each new product is performing, the team can act proactively: problems are spotted early, launch decisions get sharper, and suppliers are involved before support becomes urgent.

In the example below, a company increased its success rate for new products from **62% to 88% in 12 months.**
