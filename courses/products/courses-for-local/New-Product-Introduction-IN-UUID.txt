index_title: What Happens After Launch?
title: What Happens After Launch?
type: main
layout: standard-2-col
---
Introducing a new product is only the beginning. Once it is in the portfolio, we need to find out whether it actually develops into a strong product.

Exact failure rates vary by industry and by how failure is measured, but estimates often place new product failure at around **30 to 50 percent,** with some industries reporting even higher rates.

That makes early follow up critical. The sooner we see that a new product is falling behind, the sooner we can decide what to do about it.

**If we do not follow up on new products, today’s new introductions can become tomorrow’s dead stock.**

[id:2/topic: NPI Framework]

slide_id: e2664aa4-81d8-49d8-b470-f2f8caeed754
index_title: How Is a New Product Performing?
title: How Is a New Product Performing?
type: main
---
In Inact, we use the NPI Framework to track how new products develop after launch. The first thing we look at is performance.

The exact criteria and thresholds can vary. Common examples include revenue, volume and actual sales compared with forecast.

Based on the chosen criteria, each new product falls into one of three groups.
![image/size:large/align:center/frame:none](products-new-product-introduction/The-Performance.png)
---

slide_id: 7d9c3c57-389c-4e79-8b28-3295d21ee31e
index_title: The First 12 Months
title: The First 12 Months
type: main
---
Performance is only one part of the picture. We also need to consider how long the product has been in the portfolio.

The NPI Framework follows new products through their first 12 months and divides them into three age groups. As the product gets older, we expect more from its performance.
![image/size:large/align:center/frame:none](products-new-product-introduction/First-12-Months.png)
---

index_title: The NPI Framework
title: The NPI Framework
type: main
---
Now we bring the two dimensions together. The framework combines a product’s age with its performance at that point in time.

As the product moves through its first 12 months, we continue to assess whether it is **On track, Promising or Needs attention.** This makes it easy to see which new products are developing as expected and which ones require action.
![image/size:large/align:center/frame:none](products-new-product-introduction/The-NPI-Framework.png)

[id:3/topic: Governance - Case]

slide_id: 712fff29-6787-428d-a052-8b9ffde0fa29
index_title: From a Black Box to a Clear Process
title: From a Black Box to a Clear Process
type: main
---
We have now seen how the NPI Framework can be used to follow new products during their first year. To make this more concrete, we will look at a real example from one company that changed how it managed new product introductions.

Before the new process was introduced, the company estimated that only 50 to 70 percent of its new products were successful. Once a product had been launched, there was limited follow up and little visibility into which new products were developing well and which were becoming a problem.

The company therefore set a target of more than 80 percent successful introductions and started by making responsibility for new products much clearer.
---
> [next] Continue -> e2b390e8-cce7-4660-a942-05e419bfdf76

slide_id: e2b390e8-cce7-4660-a942-05e419bfdf76
index_title: Clear Responsibility From the Start
title: Clear Responsibility From the Start
type: main
---
The company defined clear responsibilities for new products across three roles. Each role had its own responsibility during the introduction process and its own measures to follow.
![image/size:large/align:center/frame:none](products-new-product-introduction/Responsibilities.png)
This meant that responsibility no longer disappeared once a product had been introduced.

title: Following New Products After Launch
type: main
layout: standard-2-col
---
With clear ownership in place, the company could follow new products more closely after launch.

Products that needed attention were reviewed by the Product Coordinator and Product Manager, who agreed on what to do next. Suppliers could also be involved when needed.

The goal was to act early, before weak products developed into dead stock.
![image/size:large/align:right/frame:none](products-new-product-introduction/Follow-Up-After-Launch.png)

title: Better Decisions Before Launch

The process also changed how the company decided which products to introduce.

Knowing that every new product would be followed closely made Product Managers ask more questions before launch.

This helped the company become more selective about what entered the portfolio.

index_title: The Result
title: The Result
type: main
---
The company increased the success rate for new products from **62 percent to 88 percent.**

At the same time, weak introductions were identified earlier, Product Managers became more selective, and suppliers were involved when support was needed.

The process gave the company a clearer way to manage new products from launch to handover.
