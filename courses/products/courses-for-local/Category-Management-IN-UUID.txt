title: A Few Products Drive Most of the Profit
type: main
layout: standard-2-col
---
Most product portfolios follow a familiar pattern. A small share of products creates a large share of the result.

The 80/20 principle gives us a useful rule of thumb. Around 20% of your products often generate about 80% of the profit.

So the first question is simple: Which products are driving your performance?

Now that you know what to measure, where do we start? With thousands of products, we can’t tackle them all at once.

We need to focus our energy where it creates the biggest impact.

**Therefore, we categorize our products to find the ones that matter most.**

index_title: The Four Categories
title: The Four Categories
type: main
---
So, how do we categorize them?

We do it through two commercial lenses: **Revenue** and **Margin.**

Similar to the Double ABC Analysis, each product is assessed by its revenue and margin, and its performance on these two measures determines which category it belongs to.

![image/size:large/align:center/frame:none](products-category-management/Axes-And-Categroies-With-Icons.png)

[id:4/topic: Product Profitability]

slide_id: ea3704b0-0f8a-40c3-a1e3-b2c5f29e019d
index_title: Cost to Serve
title: Cost to Serve
type: main
layout: standard-2-col
---
It often costs more to sell a product than you think.

Say you buy a product for 100 and sell it for 150, so you made 50. But the freight in takes 10, receiving it and putting it on the shelf takes another 5, the salesperson gets a bonus of 10, and sending it out to the customer takes 5 more.

**You thought you made 50, but you actually made 20.**
![video/size:large/align:right/frame:none](https://pub-3c39fac4c588469d9bf40bf9e56bdbaf.r2.dev/Products/Profit-Margin-Breakdown.mp4)
