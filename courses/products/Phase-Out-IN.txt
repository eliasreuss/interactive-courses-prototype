title: The Long Tail
type: main
---
Over time, most portfolios build up a long tail of slow-moving and dead products.

These products take up space, tie up capital and create work across the business, even when they generate little or no sales.

And phase out is not only a purchasing responsibility. Someone made the decision to bring the product into the portfolio, so product and category owners also need to take responsibility for when it should leave.

[id:3/topic: Catch the Problem Earlier]

slide_id: 4
index_title: From Reactive to Proactive
title: From Reactive to Proactive
type: main
layout: standard-2-col
---
Dead Items helps us clean up after the problem has already happened. But ideally, we should act earlier.

A product can be a poor performer long before sales reach zero. If we can identify these products earlier, we can decide what to do before they become dead stock.

But potential phase-out candidates are harder to judge than dead items. A product may have low revenue and poor margins, but somebody is still buying it. If that customer is important to the business, removing the product could have bad consequences.
![image/size:large/align:right/frame:none](placeholder-images/Illustration.png)
