Incoterms — Danish: Incoterms
Incoterms are a set of standardised delivery terms issued by the International Chamber of Commerce, the ICC. They define where responsibility for the goods passes from seller to buyer, who pays freight, insurance and duty, and who carries the risk along the way.
What an Incoterm settles
Where delivery is considered to have taken place
Who arranges and pays for transport
Who bears the risk of damage or loss on each leg of the journey
Who handles export and import customs clearance
The most common in practice
EXW, ex works, where the buyer collects at the seller's premises and carries everything from there
FOB, free on board, where the seller's responsibility ends once the goods are loaded on the vessel
CIF, where the seller pays freight and insurance to the destination port, but risk has still passed at loading
DAP, where the seller delivers to an agreed place but the buyer handles import clearance
DDP, where the seller delivers everything paid, including duty and taxes
Why it matters for inventory management
The Incoterm determines when the goods are genuinely yours — and therefore when they should count in inventory and in tied-up capital. Two suppliers at the same price and the same transit time can cost very differently once freight, insurance and duty are allocated differently.
The Incoterm also shapes real lead time. Under EXW your lead time only starts once you have arranged collection, and delays in your own transport booking are your problem, not the supplier's.
Note that Incoterms do not govern title to the goods, payment terms, or what happens on breach of contract. Those belong in the contract.
