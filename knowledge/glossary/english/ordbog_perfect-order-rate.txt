Perfect Order Rate — Danish: Perfect order rate
Perfect order rate is the share of orders completed without a fault anywhere — right item, right quantity, on time, undamaged, with correct documentation and invoicing.
How does it differ from OTIF?
OTIF measures two things: did it arrive on time, and did it arrive complete. Perfect order rate adds the rest of the customer experience — that the goods were undamaged, the paperwork correct, and the invoice right. An order can therefore be OTIF and still not perfect.
How is it calculated?
Error-free orders divided by total orders. Because it compounds several conditions that must all hold, it drops quickly. Four sub-measures at 95% each produce a perfect order rate of roughly 81%.
That is precisely the point: the figure exposes how much is lost between sub-measures that look fine in isolation.
What is it used for?
To see total quality through the customer's eyes rather than the department's. The warehouse measures picking accuracy, transport measures lead time, and finance measures invoice errors — each can look healthy while the customer experiences something wrong with every fifth order.
What does it require?
That individual fault types are recorded, not just counted in total. The value is in the breakdown: is it the documentation, the quantity or the date that fails most often? Without that breakdown, perfect order rate is just a low number nobody can act on.
