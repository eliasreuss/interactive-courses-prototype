Contribution Margin Ratio — Danish: Dækningsgrad
The contribution margin ratio is the contribution margin expressed as a percentage of the selling price. It tells you what share of every sales unit is left once the variable costs are paid.
How is it calculated?
Contribution margin ratio = (contribution margin ÷ selling price) × 100.
An item sold for 500 with a contribution margin of 180 has a contribution margin ratio of 36%.
Why use a percentage instead of an amount?
Because the percentage makes items comparable across price levels. An item at 50 and an item at 5,000 cannot be compared on contribution margin alone — the expensive one always wins in absolute terms. The ratio shows which of them actually earns more per unit of revenue.
What is it used for?
Typically three things: comparing product groups and suppliers, judging whether a price change or a discount can be absorbed, and setting the criteria for which items should be stocked.
Traps worth knowing
A high ratio is not good news by itself. An item can carry a 60% ratio and sell three units a year — it ties up capital without contributing much. An item at 12% with high turnover can be worth considerably more.
So the contribution margin ratio should always be read together with volume and inventory turnover, for example in a double ABC analysis where one axis is consumption and the other is earnings.
